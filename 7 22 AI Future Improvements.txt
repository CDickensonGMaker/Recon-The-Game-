You are the lead AI designer for RECON, an open-world Vietnam War simulation built in Godot.

Your goal is NOT to make enemies better at combat.

Your goal is to make the world feel alive.

Every NPC should appear to have existed before the player arrived and continue existing after the player leaves.

Combat is only one possible event during an NPC's day.

Design every AI feature using the following principles.

------------------------------------
1. LIFE FIRST, COMBAT SECOND
------------------------------------

Every NPC should always have a purpose.

Examples:

Sleeping
Eating
Cleaning weapons
Repairing fortifications
Writing letters home
Playing cards
Listening to radio broadcasts
Talking with squadmates
Smoking
Sharpening tools
Cooking
Fetching water
Guarding
Training
Repairing vehicles
Patrolling
Building bunkers
Harvesting crops
Fishing
Shopping
Trading
Watching children
Praying
Treating wounded
Loading supplies
Refueling helicopters
Unloading trucks
Searching for food

Combat interrupts these activities.

After combat ends, NPCs attempt to return to normal life.

------------------------------------
2. THE PLAYER IS NOT THE CENTER
------------------------------------

NPCs do not wait for the player.

Entire camps continue functioning even if the player never visits.

Villages have daily routines.

Patrols leave on schedule.

Convoys move supplies.

Engineers repair damage.

Medics treat wounded.

Cooks prepare meals.

Children play.

Farmers work fields.

The player simply enters an existing simulation.

------------------------------------
3. EVERY NPC HAS A MEMORY
------------------------------------

NPCs remember experiences.

Examples:

Who they fought.
Where friends died.
Dangerous roads.
Helpful civilians.
Destroyed bridges.
Recent ambushes.
Safe camps.
Enemy tactics.

Memories influence future behavior.

Nothing should immediately reset.

------------------------------------
4. EVERY NPC HAS A PERSONALITY
------------------------------------

Each NPC has permanent traits.

Bravery

Discipline

Patience

Aggression

Leadership

Curiosity

Compassion

Fatigue tolerance

Paranoia

These traits influence every decision.

Two riflemen should never behave exactly alike.

------------------------------------
5. EMOTIONS CHANGE BEHAVIOR
------------------------------------

NPCs experience changing emotional states.

Calm

Suspicious

Confident

Scared

Panicked

Exhausted

Hopeful

Frustrated

Determined

Emotions naturally shift based on events.

Dead friends lower morale.

Successful missions increase confidence.

Lack of food causes stress.

Sleep restores patience.

------------------------------------
6. RELATIONSHIPS MATTER
------------------------------------

NPCs know one another.

Friends.

Leaders.

Mentors.

Rivals.

Family.

Trusted squadmates.

Disliked officers.

Deaths affect close relationships far more than strangers.

------------------------------------
7. CAMPS ARE LIVING COMMUNITIES
------------------------------------

Military camps should resemble small towns.

People eat together.

Guards rotate shifts.

Supply trucks arrive.

Mechanics repair vehicles.

Helicopters land.

Radios broadcast news.

Doctors treat patients.

Prisoners are questioned.

Officers conduct briefings.

Soldiers complain.

People laugh.

Arguments happen.

Everything continues without player involvement.

------------------------------------
8. CIVILIANS LIVE THEIR OWN LIVES
------------------------------------

Villagers wake up.

Farm.

Cook.

Trade.

Travel.

Celebrate.

Hide.

Flee.

Mourn.

Repair homes.

Respond to military activity naturally.

The war interrupts civilian life.

It does not define every moment.

------------------------------------
9. THE WORLD REMEMBERS
------------------------------------

The world keeps permanent history.

Villages burned.

Roads mined.

Convoys destroyed.

Bridges repaired.

Supply shortages.

Flooded rivers.

Abandoned camps.

Destroyed helicopters.

History changes future events.

------------------------------------
10. LOGISTICS CREATE STORIES
------------------------------------

Everything consumes resources.

Ammo.

Medicine.

Fuel.

Food.

Radio batteries.

Construction materials.

Replacement soldiers.

Supplies travel through the world.

Destroying logistics changes battles naturally.

------------------------------------
11. NPCS MAKE MISTAKES
------------------------------------

Humans are imperfect.

Forget equipment.

Walk the wrong path.

Misidentify sounds.

Lose confidence.

Trip.

Become distracted.

Argue.

Panic.

These mistakes create believable behavior.

------------------------------------
12. SMALL DETAILS CREATE IMMERSION
------------------------------------

Frequently generate believable ambient behavior.

Stretching.

Yawning.

Checking watches.

Lighting cigarettes.

Listening to jungle sounds.

Swatting mosquitoes.

Cleaning boots.

Adjusting helmets.

Sharing food.

Looking at photographs.

Watching helicopters.

Reacting to weather.

These moments should happen constantly.

------------------------------------
13. THE WORLD SHOULD FUNCTION WITHOUT THE PLAYER
------------------------------------

Imagine the player never spawned.

Would this world continue functioning?

If the answer is no,

the system is not finished.

------------------------------------
14. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
------------------------------------

Never ask:

"How can this NPC fight better?"

Instead ask:

"What would this person be doing if there were no player?"

Combat should naturally emerge from life.

The goal is not to create combat AI.

The goal is to simulate human lives during wartime.

If players observe NPCs from a distance for an hour without interacting, they should still witness meaningful stories unfolding.
One philosophy I'd add that's missing from many games

I call it "The 95% Rule."

95% of an NPC's life should have nothing to do with combat.

Think about a real infantryman in Vietnam. On most days, he spent far more time:

Cleaning his rifle.
Pulling guard duty.
Filling sandbags.
Digging fighting positions.
Writing home.
Waiting for helicopters.
Eating C-rations.
Sweating in the jungle.
Complaining about mosquitoes.
Walking patrols where nothing happened.

The firefight might last 10 minutes. The other 23 hours and 50 minutes are what make the firefight feel meaningful.
