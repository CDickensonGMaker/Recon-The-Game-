# CONQUEST OF WORMS — Treatment for the Game Version

**What this is.** A writing document. The author asked to *"bring my comic to life but extend the full story in this version and tune up the apoc now."* This proposes how — the register, and three different ways the story could finish — **from his own material only**. It proposes no systems, schedules no work, and touches nothing but itself. His own ruling stands: **all of this is post-demo-launch.**

**Sources.** `production/CONQUEST_OF_WORMS_BIBLE.md` (what is on the page, read 2026-09-09) and `production/CONQUEST_OF_WORMS_AUTHOR_SYNOPSIS.md` (what he has said, verbatim). Where this document proposes something new it says so in bold and gives the drawn or told thing it grows out of.

**The line this document will not cross.** The comic stops with Eugene screaming on a plane and the first full look at the sniper. Everything past that is **proposal, with reasons and costs attached, offered so he can choose** — not an ending written on his behalf. The questions in §5 are his alone and this document deliberately leaves them open.

---

## 1 · THE APOCALYPSE NOW READING — tested

He named a register. The question is whether he already writes in it. **The honest answer is: half, and the half that is true is the better half.** Both halves matter, because importing the wrong one would cost him the thing that makes his story his.

### Where his pages already are Apocalypse Now — and it is not vague

**1. The dugout is a Kurtz compound, and he drew it before anyone said the word.** Issue 4 `fya.22`–`23`: a hole in the ground whose mouth is ringed with human skulls, whose lintel is hung with a row of captured American helmets, with a hammer-and-sickle flag inside and a **column of porters bringing him food**. That is not a sniper hide. That is a man who has stepped outside every army in the war and acquired a following that feeds him. The helmets are Kurtz's heads on posts, and the fruit basket is the more damning detail — somebody is *supplying* him.

**2. The book ends on arrival, not on battle.** Issue 4's last page is a reveal, and nothing happens in it. He is sitting down, cleaning a rifle. The film's ending is a room, and so is his.

**3. The structure of the last three issues is a descent by degrees.** Firebase → patrol → the bush → a man in a hole. Each issue is further from the wire and further from the army's writ.

**4. The tonal mix is the film's mix, and this is the strongest evidence.** Documentary procedure — burning shit barrels, the chow line, det cord on the LZ, the M16 immediate-action drill on the title page — with hallucination cutting straight into it and no seam. Do Lung Bridge is a documentary sequence that goes wrong; so is Issue 1 pp. 16–17.

**5. The absurd institution is already on the page.** Operation Wandering Soul — the army flying ghost recordings over the trees to frighten a superstitious enemy — is the tiger, the surfboard, the USO show. Real, documented, and insane. He found it himself and put it in Issue 3 p16.

**6. The narration voice is already Willard's.** First person, past tense, tired, and reporting rather than emoting: *"I've been in Vietnam for a little over two months now. Still a green grunt fresh off the American assembly line."* That is the film's voiceover register, arrived at independently.

**7. The trophies rhyme, which is the film's central move.** Gus wears ears; the sniper hangs helmets. The thing hunting you and the thing happening to your friend are the same act.

### Where his story is NOT Apocalypse Now — and importing it would break something

**1. There is no assignment, no river and no journey.** Willard is sent to terminate. Michael is a replacement doing patrols in an AO he did not choose and cannot leave. **The engine of the film is a mission upriver, and the comic has nothing like it.** Bolting one on would turn an open Vietnam into a corridor, which is the one thing RECONgame's Pillar 3 exists to prevent, and would falsify the comic besides — its subject is a man who has no agency over where he goes.

**2. The sniper is not Kurtz, and the difference is the whole point.** Kurtz is American — the army's own logic followed to its end, which is why Willard recognises himself in him. The sniper is **Russian, from a different war, made by a German, released by a Frenchman**. He is not what Michael's institution produces. He is what Michael's *grandfather* produced. That is not a critique of empire; it is a **curse**, and it is closer to Weird War Tales than to Conrad. **Call the sniper Kurtz and you replace an inherited-consequence story with a critique-of-America story — and the causal chain, which is the whole spine, becomes decoration.**

**3. His Kurtz is already in the squad, and it is Gus.** One of ours, who goes past. Ears, then a head in a bag, then eating an arm in front of an officer, then exile, then **a pistol fired point-blank into an officer's face in the middle of a chow line** (`fya.12`, drawn). That descent is the film's subject and the comic performs it on a man the player eats and sleeps beside. **Gus is the Kurtz; the sniper is something older and stranger.** Any adaptation that gets this backwards throws away the best material he has drawn.

And the `fya.12` page settles the register question on its own terms — **but only once the face on it is read correctly.** The largest horror image in the sequence sits beside the gunshot, on the same page, not after it, and the man in it is **weeping, not melting** (the author's correction, 2026-09-09; verified — tear tracks running from both lower lids, facial structure intact).

That distinction decides what kind of Kurtz Gus is. **A melting man has become something else and is therefore finished as a character.** A crying man is still entirely present and knows exactly what he has just done — which is worse, and is the only version consistent with the author's own rule that the image measures the mind. Apocalypse Now externalises Kurtz's horror into a monologue that explains it. **This book puts it in the shooter's face at the instant of the act, calls it grief, and never explains it at all.** That is the better instinct and it is already his.

**Consequence for every continuation below:** Gus must remain legible as a person to the last panel he appears in. The moment an adaptation renders him as a monster — visually or mechanically — it has made the mistake this page corrects.

**4. There is no monologue, no philosophy, and no self-aware villain.** The sniper does not explain himself. The single most effective thing he does in three issues is watch a patrol cross a stream and not fire (Issue 3 p13). Give him a speech and you lose him.

**5. The comic's stated ancestors are the right ones.** The 'NAM for the procedure, Weird War Tales for the sting. Both are *short-form* traditions — a grunt's week, an eight-page horror story with a turn at the end. His book is built out of those units, not out of a three-hour arc.

### Verdict

**He already wrote in the register and did not need to be told.** Take the compound, the arrival-not-victory ending, the absurd institution, the weary first-person voice, and the descent. **Do not take the assignment, the river, Kurtz-as-mirror, or the monologue.** RECONgame's own authored-threshold canon says the useful half of this already — *"Willard does not fly the Ride of the Valkyries; he watches it from a boat."* Michael watches. Keep him watching.

**One line worth writing on the wall of whoever adapts this:** *Gus is the man who goes; the sniper is the debt that comes.* Those are two different horrors and the book is better for having both.

## 2 · WHAT THE GAME VERSION IS TRYING TO BE

Before proposing endings, the thing they all have to serve.

**The comic's argument, as drawn:** war eats down the generations, and the horror is always somebody's mind rather than a monster in the world. Every horror image in 88 pages belongs to a specific person and measures him. The book's only external corroboration is a French nurse in 1915 confirming that the officer a boy talked to for hours had been dead for months.

**What only a game can add, and it is the author's own observation:** a comic can *show* you the chain; a game can make you the *cause* of it. His three-POV idea — Louie, the burned Russian, Michael — puts the player's own finger on every link. He said it first and it is the strongest structural idea anyone has had about this material.

**So the extension's job is not to invent an ending. It is to answer one question:** what does the story do with a player who has been all three men, or with a player who has only been one? Every proposal below is a different answer to that.

## 3 · THREE CONTINUATIONS

Each is rooted in something drawn or told. Each names what it costs and what it makes impossible.

**What is actually drawn, corrected 2026-09-09.** Far more of Issue 4 is on the page than the synopsis implies, and an earlier version of this document repeated a mistake of mine. **Drawn:** Eugene's return out of the bush to the wire (`fya.07`) · the stretcher and the infirmary (`fya.08`) · the men refusing to sit with him (`fya.11`) · the crowd, **the shot — hand-lettered "BLAM", the head bursting — and the enormous weeping face beside it** (`fya.12`) · the body in the mud (`fya.13`) · the nurse, including a hand at her waist and her freckles turning to pocks (`fya.14`) · the captivity and the bamboo cage (`fya.15`) · the airfield and the loading (`fya.18`) · the sniper's compound and full reveal (`fya.22`–`23`).

**Still told-not-drawn, and treated below as raw material rather than canon:** the rumour that he was eating people (the act itself is drawn back in Issue 3 p18, but its spread as rumour is not) · the *theft* of the pistol · the victim's rank — "the major" is the synopsis's word, and no insignia is legible on the page · the sniper "starts to torture on the squad" · the female revolutionary, who has no page anywhere.

---

### CONTINUATION A — "THE HELMET WALL"
*The smallest thing that completes the story. Vietnam only, one point of view.*

**Shape.** Pick up where Issue 4's drawn pages stop. Gus goes out on the plane. Then the author's own next sentence — *"starts to torture on the squad"* — becomes the body of the story, and the comic has already drawn the method: **a rifle in the foreground that does not fire** (Issue 3 p13). The escalation is not more shooting. It is *fewer* shootings and more absences: men who do not come back, a poncho with nobody under it, a helmet on the wire in the morning. The squad's problem is that nothing is happening to them and they are being reduced anyway.

**The ending, proposed.** The squad finds the dugout. There is nobody in it. There is a wall of American helmets, some of which they recognise, and a hammer-and-sickle flag, and a cold fire. **The story ends on arrival, exactly as Issue 4 already ends on arrival** — the question is answered and there is nothing to kill. Michael walks back out to the wire with his tour clock still running.

**Rooted in:** I3 p13 (the watcher who does not fire) · I3 p22 (the taking of Gus) · I4 `fya.15` (the cage — a man who takes prisoners rather than kills) · I4 `fya.22`–`23` (the compound) · I1 p19 (*"Only 320 days left for ya?"*) · the author's "torture on the squad."

**Costs.** By far the cheapest. One era, one art kit, the cast that exists, no WW1, no WW2. It is a Vietnam game with a ghost story in it.

**Forecloses.** **The causal chain never pays off.** Louie's mercy stays a thing the reader was told and Michael never learns; the generational thesis — the title itself — remains subtext. It also forecloses the three-POV structure permanently, because once the Vietnam story has ended, going back to build 1915 and 1943 is a sequel, not a structure.

**Honest note.** This is the version most likely to actually get finished, and the version that leaves the most of his best idea on the table.

---

### CONTINUATION B — "THE CHAIN"
*His three-POV idea, carried through to an ending. Three wars.*

**Shape.** The player is Louie in French service, then the Russian, then Michael — the structure in the synopsis. The extension's contribution is the **third act**, because the first two are already his: the mercy, and the burning.

**The proposal, and it is the load-bearing one: Michael never learns the connection. The player does.** The journal is the only object that crosses eras and it stops in 1918; nothing in it names a scarred German boy's future. So Michael reads his grandfather's war, is hunted by its consequence, and dies or goes home without ever knowing why. **The dramatic irony belongs entirely to the player, which is the one thing this medium can do with this story and no other medium can.** It also costs nothing — it is a decision not to write a revelation scene.

**Two endings that fit, both rooted:**

- **The mercy comes back down the chain.** The sniper has Michael and does not fire — the same beat as Issue 3 p13, and the same act as *"Run..."* in Issue 3 p5, one generation later and pointing the other way. Neither man knows what he is repeating. **Rooted in two drawn panels; requires no new idea at all.** Its risk: it is tidy, and this author's instincts run against tidy.
- **The sniper is simply outlived.** Michael's tour ends. The sniper is still there. Nobody wins, nobody is avenged, and the last image is the compound with somebody else's helmet newly on the wall. **Rooted in the tour clock and the book's habit of killing men in the gutter** — Sgt. Maddox dies in a subordinate clause, and that restraint is the tone.

**Rooted in:** the full causal chain in the synopsis · I3 p5 (*"Run..."*, and the scar) · I3 p13 · I4 `fya.23` (the Soviet flag, which is the only page-level evidence of the Russian identity) · the WW2 section being *"not a front, it is a room"* — his own scoping.

**Costs.** Three art kits, two of which do not exist anywhere: WW1 poilu — Adrian helmet, horizon-blue, Lebel or Berthier, Chauchat — and WW2 Eastern Front. His own mitigation is correct and should be kept: **the Russian's section is a capture and a burning, an interior and a handful of men, not a campaign.**

**Forecloses.** A tight Vietnam-only game. And irreversibly: **playing the Russian humanises him.** After the player has been inside that face, the sniper can never be pure menace again. That may be the point — the synopsis argues it is — but it cannot be undone, and it means Continuation A's ending (arrival at an empty compound belonging to a thing you never met) is no longer available.

---

### CONTINUATION C — "THE TWO BOOKS"
*The middle path. Vietnam is the game; WW1 is the journal; WW2 is a face.*

**Shape.** Michael's Vietnam is the whole game — patrols, the firebase, Gus's decline, the sniper. WW1 exists exactly as the author described the transition: he sits on his bunk, opens the journal, and is in 1915. Louie's sections are **short, authored and closed** — the trench and the burial detail, the crater with Durand, and the patrol that ends with the young German. Three sequences, not a front. **WW2 never appears as a playable era at all.** The burning exists only as what is written on the sniper's face, plus what the Russian's own hoard says about him — Soviet flag, foreign rifle, no army.

**The ending, proposed.** Louie's last journal entry and Michael's last day are cut against each other, the way Issue 2 pp. 16–17 already cut French shells falling on French troops straight into a skeleton standing in the Vietnamese bush. **The comic has already demonstrated its own ending's grammar; this just uses it once more.** What each man does in his final sequence is §5's question, not this document's.

**Rooted in:** the journal-as-transition (his, verbatim) · I2 p5 (the object producing the cut) · I2 pp.16→17 (the cut made on a rhyme, not a line) · I3 pp.3–7 (the crater — the one sequence in the comic that is genuinely better in first person than on paper) · I3 p5.

**Costs.** Two kits, the second small and reusable — trench modules over cut terrain. Middle of the three by a wide margin.

**Forecloses.** **The player never performs the burning, so the chain is known rather than suffered.** The synopsis's best argument — *"only a game can make you the cause of it"* — is half-honoured: you give the mercy, you never pay for it. That is a real loss and should be weighed honestly rather than talked around.

## 4 · SEVEN BEATS THAT SHOULD EXIST IN ANY VERSION

Rooted in drawn pages, and independent of which continuation is chosen.

1. **A rifle that watches and does not fire.** Issue 3 p13. Whatever else happens, the sniper's introduction is a threat that declines to be one. Everything about him should be built outward from this panel.
2. **The trophy rhyme, uncommented.** Gus's ear necklace and the helmet wall in the same story, with nobody remarking on it. The comic makes this connection entirely in pictures and it must not be explained.
3. **Gus's turn on one page.** *"Is that what were supposed to be doing?"* and, one page later, the ears. The gap between the question and the mutilation is a single page turn and that speed is the point — a man does not decay slowly here, he is *praised* and then he is gone.
4. **The veterans' justification, verbatim.** Issue 2 p23. *"Sometimes taking a little piece of Charlie home is the only we HQ can even confirm a kill, Baby!"* The horror is institutional before it is personal, and he already wrote the dialogue.
5. **A death in the gutter.** Somebody important dies in a subordinate clause, off-page, the way Sgt. Maddox does. If every death is staged, the tone is gone.
6. **One external corroboration, and only one.** The 1915 nurse is the single moment in 88 pages when the world agrees with a man's vision. **Whatever version is built, it should get exactly one of these, and no more.** The restraint is what keeps the horror psychological.
7. **The paperwork.** Draft cards, the PS Magazine maintenance art, the rabbit-skinning manual. Official documents are this book's connective tissue and its title pages are already designed.

## 5 · THE QUESTIONS THAT ARE HIS ALONE

Not answered here, deliberately. Several are named as open in his own synopsis and this document does not close them.

**About the ending**
1. **What happens to Gus?** He goes out on a plane, screaming, strapped down. Does the story follow him, or is the plane the last we see of him? Following him is a second story about a hospital and a court-martial — **he shot an officer in front of witnesses, and that page is drawn** — while not following him is the more brutal and more period-accurate choice. **This is the biggest single fork in the extension**, and Issue 4's drawn pages already carry it to the aircraft door and no further.
2. **Is the sniper confronted, escaped, or simply outlived?** Three genuinely different books. The pages support all three and prefer none.
3. **Does Michael go home?** The tour clock is drawn — *"Only 320 days left for ya?"* — so the story has a natural end date that is not a victory. Whether he reaches it is his call.
4. **Does Michael ever learn what his grandfather did?** This document argues he should not, and that the player should carry the knowledge alone. **That is an argument, not a decision.**

**About the chain**
5. **Can the player, as Louie, actually shoot the young German — and what happens if he does?** Named in the synopsis as the hardest question in the adaptation, and it still is. If he cannot, the mercy is scenery. If he can, the story needs a second spine.
6. **Is the Russian playable, a scene, or panel-only?** His words, his call. The cost note stands: his section is a room, not a front.
7. **Does the female revolutionary get built?** She is the one planned character with no drawn page anywhere, and she is the only Vietnamese-side principal in the entire conception. **Without her, every named antagonist in the story is European and the Vietnamese are terrain.** That is worth him knowing before he decides, and it is not this document's decision to make.

**About register**
8. **Is Gus the Kurtz, as §1 argues?** If yes, the sniper stops being the climax and becomes the inheritance, and the last act belongs to the man in your own squad rather than the man in the hole.
