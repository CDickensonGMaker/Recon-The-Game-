# GAME-DESIGNER — Independent Sight
**Session:** 2026-08-18 Tet/Hue operations option space · **Lens:** player experience & fun (Rule One)
**Mode per briefing: BREADTH.** This is an option catalog with tradeoffs, not a recommendation.

---

## 0 · Position on the fiction↔history dial (required by briefing §3)

The dial has five detents. The player-experience question is: *what does the player get to believe?*

| Detent | What it is | What the player feels | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Pure fiction** (7/28 decree as-ruled) | Invented province, invented units, "Vietnam-flavored" | Total freedom; no player ever says "that's not how Hue went" | Sacrifices the single most electric word in the pitch: **Tet**. "A big attack happens" lands softer than "Tet is coming." |
| **B. Historical weather, fictional ground** ⭐ my position | Real WAR calendar (Tet, Jan 31 1968, the Hue fight raging *offscreen/nearby*), but OUR firebase, OUR fictional AO, fictional unit. Hue is real and close; you are the invented outfit next door. | "I was *near* Hue during Tet" — the exact grunt experience: history happened 8 klicks away and its shrapnel landed on you. Radio carries real-war news; your fights stay authored. | You never fight *in* the Citadel proper. Some players will want the postcard battle and we withhold it. |
| **C. Fictional twin city** | A "Hue-like" imperial city (walled citadel, river, canals) with a fictional name, sited in our world | City fighting without historical liability; we can burn it, lose it, retake it per OUR campaign | Players see through the rename instantly ("that's Hue with a hat on") — can read as cowardice rather than craft. Art cost of a citadel either way. |
| **D. Named-battle ride-alongs** | Home AO fictional, but handcrafted operations are LABELED as real actions ("relief column to Hue, Feb 1968") | Maximum goosebumps per operation; the COD-style "you were there" hit | Collides hardest with the 7/28 "no real dates, units or dead" ruling; invites accuracy litigation; the not-the-hero doctrine strains when the level is a famous victory. |
| **E. Full re-enactment** | Historical units, dates, order of battle | — | Off the table by decree; kills the seeded-tour premise; named only for completeness. |

**My read:** B is the sweet spot, with C as the amendment if he wants a walkable citadel. B costs the least canon (it amends "no real dates" only — units and dead stay fictional) and buys the most: **Tet as a date the player dreads is a fear-doctrine feature.** D can be layered on later per-operation if a specific map earns it.

## 1 · The patrol↔operation rhythm

The unit of pacing is **the player's week at the firebase**. Options, escalating in structure:

### R1 — Pure opt-in ("the board is the bird")
Ops are rumors: the RTO mentions a unit in contact, a Huey is spinning up on the pad, you walk over and get on. Never ordered. Skip forever if you want.
- **Fun:** perfect fit with "leave the camp and go find problems" — the op IS a problem you found. Boarding a helicopter because you *chose to* is a stronger fantasy than being sent.
- **Tradeoff:** players who never wander to the pad never see the handcrafted maps we spent months on. Zero pacing authority — the game cannot guarantee its best content gets seen.

### R2 — Ordered rotation ("you're a soldier, act like one")
The radio periodically ASSIGNS you: "Recon element, be on the pad at 0600." Refusing isn't offered.
- **Fun:** the truest grunt fantasy — you don't pick your war. Anticipatory dread ("op tomorrow") is free atmosphere. The night before an op becomes a mood.
- **Tradeoff:** violates the open-sim north star's spirit (no rails, ever — Pillar 3). An order the player can't refuse is a rail with a radio voice. Mid-patrol orders would yank agency at the worst moment.

### R3 — Ordered-but-porous ⭐ (the hybrid I'd argue for)
Ops are assigned diegetically with LEAD TIME ("pad at first light tomorrow") — but the world doesn't hard-stop you. Miss the bird and the op happens without you; the radio carries how it went; the casualty ledger and stature remember. Catch the next one or don't.
- **Fun:** orders create rhythm and dread; consequences replace rails. "The op I skipped went bad" is a Fail Forward story (Pillar 5). The firebase feels like an Army, not a quest hub.
- **Tradeoff:** we must actually simulate/narrate the op you skipped (radio traffic, returning wounded, ledger deltas) or the porousness is fake. That's real work; a faked version is worse than R2.

### R4 — Stature-gated ("earn the seat")
Ops unlock via faction stature / rank: green privates walk patrols; proven soldiers get picked for the big ones.
- **Fun:** progression fantasy; makes patrols FEED ops ("do well out there and they'll take you to Hue"). Ties the two loops into one economy.
- **Tradeoff:** gates the marquee content behind grind; a struggling player may never ride the bird. Also inverts realism — in 1968 the green kid got sent first.

### Frequency / cadence options (orthogonal to the above)
- **Metronome:** ~1 op per 2–3 in-game days. Predictable, digestible, risks feeling like a mission table wearing a radio.
- **Clustered:** quiet weeks then 3 ops in 4 days when the war surges. Matches how offensives actually feel; pairs perfectly with a Tet clock (§2). Tradeoff: quiet stretches must be *interesting* quiet or they read as content drought.
- **Reactive:** ops triggered by what YOUR patrols stir up (you found the camp → tomorrow's op is the air assault ON it). Strongest possible loop-weld — the sandbox writes the set-piece's reason. Tradeoff: handcrafted maps can't literally be the camp you found; needs a light fiction ("the camp you found" plays on the handcrafted camp map) and players may notice the seam.

**The RTO as the interface:** all of these run through one diegetic channel — the radio watch. Options for delivery flavor: overheard traffic that foreshadows ("Alpha's in the shit at the river") → warning order that evening → the pad at dawn. Foreshadow-then-assign is the COD trailer moment done diegetically, and it's cheap: it's voice lines and a schedule.

## 2 · Tet as a playable idea — what the date DOES to the player

Options, roughly stackable rather than exclusive:

### T1 — Tet as backdrop (ambient only)
The campaign is simply SET in Jan–Feb 1968: radio news, holiday references, distant city glow, higher baseline enemy activity. No mechanical event.
- **Fun:** cheapest possible flavor win; every existing system just gets a coat of 1968 paint.
- **Tradeoff:** wastes the word. "Tet" as wallpaper is the one option that gets us nothing mechanical.

### T2 — Tet as THE CLOCK (campaign builds toward a known night) ⭐ biggest single idea in this session
The campaign starts in early January. The player learns — through radio intel, captured documents (bodies give intel!), prisoner interrogations, patrol discoveries (arms caches moving, trails widening) — that *something* is coming around the holiday. Then, on the night, EVERYWHERE goes loud at once: the home firebase catches its own siege (we own that tech — SiegeDirector) while the radio net collapses into a hundred simultaneous fights.
- **Fun:** this is the fear doctrine at campaign scale. Dread with a DATE. Every patrol before it becomes evidence-gathering ("the caches are real — it's coming"). The demo's siege night becomes the campaign's crescendo instead of its whole life.
- **Leans on:** SiegeDirector + MarchingCell (proven), casualty ledger, bodies-give-intel-only ruling (finally load-bearing at campaign scale!), the day/night clock, radio/RTO.
- **Tradeoff:** a scripted date inside a seeded sandbox is a rail — a soft one (you can be anywhere when it hits, and *where you are that night* is your story), but the purists' objection must be named. Also: everything after the climax risks feeling like epilogue; needs a post-Tet arc (the counteroffensive = the operations surge, see T4).

### T3 — Tet as intensity dial (systemic surge, no fixed date)
No calendar; instead a campaign-long escalation curve (or ledger-driven surge) that at some point tips into an offensive-scale week. Different campaigns tip at different times.
- **Fun:** preserves seeded-tour purity; replayable ("my Tet came in week 2").
- **Tradeoff:** loses the historical dread of a KNOWN date — and the known date is exactly what Tet uniquely offers over "a big attack." Also harder to author foreshadowing that lands.

### T4 — Tet as the operations faucet
Before the offensive: ops are sparse, routine (LZ security, firebase construction). After Tet night: the handcrafted op menu floods open — relief columns, recapture fights, city blocks — because now the whole corps is in contact. The offensive is WHY operations exist.
- **Fun:** solves "why does my quiet firebase keep getting invited to huge battles" — the fiction and the content cadence become the same curve. Clustered cadence (§1) gets its justification.
- **Tradeoff:** front-loads the campaign with its quietest content; new players meet the patrols-only game first. (Arguably correct — boredom-then-terror is the tonal north star — but it's a retention risk and must be named.)

### T5 — Tet night AT HOME as the mandatory beat
Whatever else is optional, the firebase's own Tet night siege is the one event every player gets, because they live there. The demo already IS this night — the campaign recontextualizes it.
- **Fun:** the strongest possible reuse story: our most-polished 30 minutes becomes the campaign's centerpiece rather than a one-off.
- **Tradeoff:** players who played the demo have seen the shape; the campaign version needs escalation (bigger, longer, or personal — YOUR named squad on the wire).

**Stack I'd sketch for the talk:** T2 + T4 + T5 with T1's dressing — a dated clock, foreshadowed by patrol intel, detonating at home, then opening the op faucet for the February counteroffensive arc. T3 held as the "purist mode" alternative.

## 3 · Operation type menu, 1968 / Tet / Hue flavored

Format per type: **fantasy · 10-second pitch · leans on · tradeoff**. His four first, then the expansion. All assume handcrafted maps (Summoner's refinement) and the assault-and-hold spine.

### His named four
1. **Heli assault on an enemy camp** — *fantasy:* the 1st Cav postcard; doors open over the treeline. *Pitch:* "Ride in with a company, take the camp before dark." *Leans on:* SiegeDirector inverted (WE assault), Huey tech, camp set pieces. *Tradeoff:* offense-AI is the unproven half of our siege tech — attacking friendly AI that looks smart is harder than defending.
2. **Hold an LZ ~20 min** — *fantasy:* Ia Drang; the perimeter that must not break. *Pitch:* "Twenty minutes till the next lift. Hold." *Leans on:* the demo siege nearly verbatim, on a new map. Cheapest op we can build. *Tradeoff:* closest to the demo = highest "I've played this" risk; needs a twist (shrinking perimeter, mid-hold casevac).
3. **Secure zones during firebase construction** — *fantasy:* watching a base get BORN; engineers work while you screen. *Pitch:* "Keep the sappers off the engineers till the berm's up." *Leans on:* destructible/constructible structures, defensive zones doctrine, garrison-soldier systems. *Tradeoff:* slow-burn op; fun depends on ambient pressure quality, not a big set piece — riskiest to make fun per hour.
4. **Seek-and-destroy** — *fantasy:* the classic hump; find them before they find you. *Pitch:* "Something's in that valley. Go make contact." *Leans on:* the entire patrol loop on a handcrafted map. *Tradeoff:* least differentiated from the free patrol game — must justify the helicopter ride with density the seeded AO can't generate.

### Tet/Hue expansion
5. **City block clearing (Hue-adjacent streets)** — *fantasy:* Full Metal Jacket's second half; the jungle war's rules stop working. *Pitch:* "Third block this morning. They're in the walls." *Leans on:* modular buildings (proven), shoot-through walls (destructible export contract — walls that eat men are ALREADY our tech), fear doctrine peaks indoors. *Tradeoff:* the named cost — indoor AI/navmesh — is our weakest system; a dumb room-clearing enemy kills the fantasy dead. Highest ceiling, highest floor-risk on the menu.
6. **Relief of a surrounded unit** — *fantasy:* being the cavalry while terrified; marching TOWARD the gunfire. *Pitch:* "Bravo's cut off at the pagoda. Get to them before dark." *Leans on:* MarchingCell as the enemy ring, friendly-AI pocket defending itself (siege tech pointed at NPCs), radio as a live countdown of their state. *Tradeoff:* the surrounded unit must credibly fight and die on its own schedule — a pocket that waits politely for the player breaks it; a pocket that dies at T+9:00 regardless is a rail.
7. **Bridge / canal fight** — *fantasy:* the one crossing that matters; fire lanes over water. *Pitch:* "Hold the bridge or drop it — either way they don't cross." *Leans on:* water-as-geometry ruling, small handcrafted map (bridges are naturally tight = cheap), explosives systems for the demolition choice. *Tradeoff:* geometry funnels = the AI's flanking brain shows poorly; a bridge fight can degenerate into a shooting gallery that violates believable-firefights.
8. **Counterattack / recapture** — *fantasy:* taking back ground that was OURS this morning — familiar spaces made hostile. *Pitch:* "They took the district compound at 0300. Take it back." *Leans on:* assault-and-hold run in reverse; if the map is a place the player has seen intact (even in one prior op), memory does the horror work free. *Tradeoff:* the payoff depends on before/after recognition, which demands map reuse across the campaign — a sequencing constraint on op scheduling.
9. **MEDCAP gone wrong** — *fantasy:* the war's other face — a village aid mission that turns. *Pitch:* "Doc's giving shots in the ville. It's quiet. It's too quiet." *Leans on:* village set, civilians, ambient-encounter tech, tension-without-contact (atmosphere pillar carries the first half). *Tradeoff:* civilians in a firefight is the atrocity-hook minefield — huge tonal payoff, and the single most design-review-intensive op on this list. Also some players will hate a 10-minute unarmed-tension front half.
10. **Convoy escort into a hot city** — *fantasy:* Battlefield's contribution — riding a truck bed through ambush country toward a burning skyline. *Pitch:* "Get the ammo to the compound. The highway's alive." *Leans on:* vehicle tech (thin today — named as a cost, not a lean), linear handcrafted corridor = cheap authoring, ambient AA/tracer dressing. *Tradeoff:* our vehicle systems are the least-built thing this op needs; also the most on-rails shape in the menu — must be dressed carefully to not feel like a theme-park ride.
11. **Night ambush op (patrol's evil twin)** — *fantasy:* YOU are the thing in the dark for once. *Pitch:* "Set the claymores on the trail. Then wait." *Leans on:* claymores, night systems, fear-doctrine inversion (the waiting is the game). *Tradeoff:* an op where nothing happens for 15 minutes is either the best atmosphere piece we ship or a refund driver; needs guaranteed contact with variable timing.
12. **Casevac under fire / litter run** — *fantasy:* the war's real scoreboard — carry a man out, not a flag. *Pitch:* "The LZ's too hot for the bird. Carry him to the river." *Leans on:* litter-team architecture (exists!), medical systems, casualty ledger identity. *Tradeoff:* movement-encumbered escort of an AI stretcher team through fire is a pathing torture test; jank here reads as comedy, the one tone we cannot survive in this op.

**Menu-shape note for the talk:** these cluster into three cost tiers — (cheap: 2, 4, 7, 11) reuse the siege/patrol spine nearly whole; (medium: 1, 3, 6, 8) need offense-AI or friendly-pocket work; (expensive: 5, 9, 10, 12) each carries a system we haven't proven (indoor AI, civilians, vehicles, litter pathing). A roadmap could ship a tier per update — Tet content cadence post-launch mirrors T4's in-fiction faucet.

## 4 · The vibe blend — COD × Battlefield × Men of Valor × HLL

What each ingredient concretely contributes, and where it fights our doctrine:

| Source | Take THIS | It shows up as | Conflict with our identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **COD** | Authored intensity: the scripted crescendo, the arrival beat, the "holy shit" skyline. Density per minute. | The helicopter ride-in (§5), op opening beats, foreshadow-then-assign radio writing, the Tet-night detonation | COD makes you the hero and makes death cheap. We may take its STAGING, never its power fantasy or its checkpoint forgiveness. A COD moment where the player is the audience, not the star, is the filter: tracers over YOUR head, not your killstreak. |
| **Battlefield** | Scale legibility: the sense that the battle is bigger than your fight — armor, air, smoke columns, other squads audibly fighting their own war | Ambient AA/tracer decree extended to ops; friendly AI elements fighting adjacent objectives we never ask the player to touch; vehicles as TERRAIN and taxis, not toys | Battlefield is a sandbox of empowerment (grab the jet). Our man never commandeers anything. Vehicles carry him or shoot near him; the moment he pilots one, not-the-hero dies. |
| **Men of Valor** | The squad as narrative: named men, banter under fire, the ride TO the fight as characterization time, period radio texture | Squad voice lines keyed to op phases; the helicopter as the dialogue scene; grief systems when a named man dies on an op | MoV is linear and cutscene-driven. We take its WRITING POSTURE (grunts talking like grunts, diegetically) and refuse its rails. Risk: heavy scripted banter on handcrafted maps drifts us toward authored-linear feel. |
| **HLL** | The weight: lethality (already ours), the terror of open ground, being one replaceable man in a formation, victory as meters gained at cost | Already our combat backbone; ops add its OPERATIONAL feel — you hold one trench of a big line; the casualty ledger as the only scoreboard | HLL is multiplayer-emergent; its magic is other humans. Solo, its weight must come from AI + atmosphere, which is exactly Pillars 1–2 — least conflict, hardest to fake. |

**The blend's internal war, named:** COD and HLL pull opposite directions on ONE axis — *how much the game promises you'll survive the next ten minutes.* COD promises yes (else its staging is wasted); HLL promises nothing. Our fear doctrine sides with HLL. So the rule I'd propose for the talk: **COD staging on the way IN, HLL physics once boots touch ground.** The ride is cinema; the LZ is the war. That seam is the design.

## 5 · The helicopter ride as an experience beat

The ride is the op's title card, tone-setter, and (technically) the load mask (GAME_GUIDE §3 already wants the Huey ride back as world-load mask). Options by ambition:

### H1 — The postcard (fixed flight, staged view)
Authored flight path over authored sights: burning ville, gun run in the distance, refugee column, the city glow on the horizon. Doors open, feet on the skids, radio chatter and squad banter timed to landmarks. 60–120 seconds.
- **Fun:** the single highest atmosphere-per-dev-hour beat available to us. Every op gets a distinct overture; the Hue-glow flyby IS the Tet marketing shot.
- **Tradeoff:** it's a rail — an honest, diegetic one (you're cargo; grunts WERE cargo — the rail is the fiction), but a rail. Replay fatigue on op #5 of the same map; needs a skip-after-first-view stance or variant chatter.

### H2 — The contested ride (systems live during flight)
Ground fire is real; tracers can hit the airframe; the door gunner (or you, see H4) actually engages real spawned enemies; the LZ can go hot and DIVERT — you land somewhere else on the map than briefed.
- **Fun:** the fear doctrine starts before landing; the divert mechanic is gold — "we're putting down short, walk the rest" regenerates the open-approach freedom (Pillar 3) INSIDE a handcrafted map, and gives one map N openings.
- **Tradeoff:** a player death during an unskippable ride they don't control is the worst death in the game — near-total loss of agency. If rides can kill, they must telegraph brutally and rarely; or damage hits the AIRCRAFT/schedule, never the player, which is a fiction we must keep consistent.

### H3 — The formation (you are one bird of many)
The lift is a company: 6–10 Hueys in trail, gunship escort weaving, other sticks visible unloading on parallel LZs when you flare in.
- **Fun:** the Battlefield contribution distilled; nothing sells "man inside the battle, not the hero" harder than watching an identical squad jump off the identical bird 80m away. Ia Drang in one image.
- **Tradeoff:** N animated helicopters + full sticks is a perf and animation bill (though Chinook/Huey work is largely paid); background birds can be lower-LOD fakes but the seam shows if one lands near you.

### H4 — The working ride (a job in the air)
You're not pure cargo: door gun for a leg, hold the map for the LT, pop smoke on the run-in, or return fire out the door when it goes loud.
- **Fun:** hands-on players stay engaged on repeat rides; the door-gun leg is a beloved genre beat (MoV did exactly this).
- **Tradeoff:** manning a gun edges toward the hero seat, and a door-gun sequence is COD's most parodied set piece — must be occasional and requested BY the crew ("get on that sixty, we're one gunner down"), never a default minigame.

### H5 — The ride home (the mirror beat, cheap and always earned)
Whatever the op, the extraction ride replays the inbound view transformed: the ville now burning, wounded on the floor, the ledger's cost visible, silence where banter was. Same flight tech, opposite emotional key.
- **Fun:** the debrief we swore never to put on a screen — done as a view out a door. Our whole "casualty ledger is the scoreboard" identity gets its cinematic. Nearly free: it's the H1 pipeline pointed backward.
- **Tradeoff:** only lands if the world state actually reads from the op outcome (smoke where you fought, an empty seat if a named man died). Faked, it's wallpaper; real, it's a small state-plumbing job per map.

**Radio/audio spine across all H-options:** the ride is where the period-HUD/diegetic doctrine pays off — nav chatter, other units' traffic bleeding through, the crew chief's two-minute call, the door gunner charging his gun. Audio is 70% of this beat and mostly a writing + mixing cost, not a tech one.

## 6 · What I want the Arbiter to weigh (positions, not just options)

1. **Fiction dial: detent B** (real war-calendar, fictional ground/unit) — the cheapest amendment that unlocks the word "Tet"; C (fictional twin city) as the extension if he wants a walkable citadel.
2. **Rhythm: R3 ordered-but-porous** — orders create the soldier fantasy; consequences (not rails) enforce them. R1's pure opt-in wastes handcrafted content; R2 breaks Pillar 3.
3. **Tet: the T2 clock is the biggest fun idea in the session** — dread with a date, foreshadowed by patrol intel, detonating as the home siege (T5), then opening the op faucet (T4). It finally makes bodies-give-intel load-bearing at campaign scale.
4. **Op menu: ship by cost tier.** LZ-hold, bridge, night ambush, seek-and-destroy are near-free on the siege/patrol spine; city clearing is the crown jewel and the biggest risk (indoor AI) — it should headline a roadmap update, not lead it.
5. **Vibe rule: COD staging inbound, HLL physics on the ground.** The helicopter ride is the licensed COD moment; the LZ is where the promise of survival is revoked.
6. **Ride: H1+H5 (postcard in, mirror out) as the baseline every op gets; H2's divert as the freedom-regenerator on handcrafted maps; H3 formation for the marquee ops; H4 sparingly on request of the crew.**

**Law 2 ledger — what my own preferred stack sacrifices:** the T2 date is a soft rail inside a sandbox that swore off rails; R3's porousness demands we simulate ops the player skips; detent B withholds the Citadel postcard some players will come for; and every handcrafted op map spends art/AI effort the seeded world can't reuse. None of it is free.
