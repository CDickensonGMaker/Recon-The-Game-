# WRITER / NARRATIVE-DIRECTOR — Independent Sight
**Session:** 2026-08-18 Tet/Hue operations · **Lens:** fiction, tone, period authenticity
**Canon read:** briefing.md · GAME_GUIDE.md §1 (tonal north star: Platoon / Hamburger Hill / Apocalypse Now; "a line grunt, not an operator") · 7/28 operations decree (fictional unit ruling) · open-sim pivot (no briefing screens, diegetic everything).

---

## 0 · The crux, stated plainly

The 7/28 decree and today's ask are not actually in conflict — they are two halves of the
correct answer that war fiction solved fifty years ago. **The EVENT can be real while the
GROUND is ours.** Platoon never names its real battle. The Things They Carried invents its
villages. Men of Valor puts you at Hue with a fictional squad. The Tet Offensive is a
historical WEATHER SYSTEM — it can roll over any firebase in I Corps, including one that
never existed. The seeded-tour premise ("every player's tour is their own") breaks only if
the PLAYER'S GROUND is real, because then every tour is the same tour. It does not break
because the radio says the truce is broken countrywide.

## 1 · The dial — full range of options

### A. Fully fictional analog (invented offensive, invented city, pure vibe)
"The Lunar Offensive" hits "Quang Loc City" in an unnamed year that smells like '68.
- **Gained:** total freedom; zero research debt; zero taste risk; the seed can do anything;
  no grognard letters about the 6th NVA Regiment being in the wrong province.
- **Lost:** almost everything the Summoner is asking for. "Tet" is one of maybe five
  Vietnam words a lay player carries in (Tet, Hue, Khe Sanh, napalm, Charlie). An invented
  offensive has to EARN the dread Tet gets for free. Fictional-analog Vietnam also reads
  as evasive in a way fictional-analog WWII doesn't — the war is recent enough that
  scrubbing its name feels like flinching. Men of Valor, Vietcong, Battlefield Vietnam
  all said "Vietnam, 1968" out loud.
- **Seeded tour:** perfectly protected — and pointlessly, since the premise was never
  threatened by the calendar, only by the map.
- **Verdict:** this is the coward's setting. It sacrifices Rule One's texture for a legal
  caution nobody needs.

### B. Historical BACKDROP, fictional foreground ★ my recommendation
Real war, real dates, real weather: it IS January 1968, the Tet truce IS broken, Hue IS
burning 30 klicks north — on the radio, in the Stars & Stripes, in the refugee columns.
But YOUR firebase, YOUR battalion, YOUR district capital are invented, seeded, ours.
The named things the player touches are fictional; the named things the player HEARS
ABOUT are real. This is the Men of Valor / Platoon / 84 Charlie MoPic register, and it is
what 90% of respected war fiction does.
- **Gained:** Tet's free dramatic capital (the broken truce, the cities, Cronkite);
  period-correct combat content justified by the calendar; the firebase sim gains a
  SEASON — pre-Tet quiet, the night it breaks, the weeks of aftershock; the horizon
  becomes a character.
- **Lost:** we take on a light research duty (the backdrop must not contradict the
  record — no B-52s that don't exist yet, no M16A2s, real radio procedure). Cheap
  compared to what it buys; most of it we already carry (period HUD decree, armory).
- **Seeded tour:** fully protected. Every player gets Tet; no player gets the same
  ground. "Everyone remembers where they were when the truce broke" is exactly how the
  real war worked — 500,000 men, each with his own Tet. The offensive is the SHARED
  CLOCK; the AO is the private map. That's not a compromise of the premise, it's its
  strongest possible expression.
- **Taste/legal:** we never claim real dead, never portray a named real soldier, never
  put the player at a site with a body count history books own (the Citadel flag, the
  Huong Giang massacre ditches). Real units may be MENTIONED on the radio net as
  background traffic ("1st Cav is moving up Highway 1") — that's newspaper truth, not
  re-enactment — but the player never wears their patch. Our battalion is invented.
- **7/28 decree status:** this is an AMENDMENT, not a violation — the decree's "without
  the real dates" clause loosens to "without the real GROUND AND DEAD; real dates are
  the backdrop." The decree's protected asset (seeded tours) survives intact; name the
  amendment and move on.

### C. Named historical operations the player rides into
Huey ops that land at real places on real days: "Operation Hue City, 12 Feb 1968, you
are attached to 2/5 Marines at the An Cuu bridge."
- **Gained:** maximum recognition and gravity; hand-crafted op maps (the Summoner's new
  architecture) technically FIT this — a consistent shared map could be "the real place."
- **Lost:** the seeded-tour premise breaks at the ops layer — every player fought the
  same named battle, and the game becomes partially a re-enactment with a right answer.
  Worse, taste: real named operations have real casualty lists. Putting a player
  score-screen (our casualty ledger IS the scoreboard) on top of a battle where 216
  actual Marines and soldiers died is a genuinely different moral object than doing so
  in a fictional battle. Re-enactment also invites the authenticity audit we cannot win
  at PSX scale: if it says Dong Ba Tower, people who know will measure us against Dong
  Ba Tower. Research burden goes from "read three books" to "defend every block."
- **Verdict:** reject for player-touched content. One exception worth debating below (§C+).

### C+. The half-step: real operation NAMES as radio wallpaper only
The player's fictional operations live inside a real named umbrella the way real units
did ("all of II Corps is under Operation MACARTHUR" — nobody on the ground felt owned by
the name). Radio and paperwork may cite the real umbrella; the player's actual op is ours.
Costs nothing, buys period smell. Compatible with B; fold it in.

### D. The spectrum knob (for completeness)
These aren't exclusive eras of the game — the dial can sit at B for the home AO and
drift toward C-flavored *fiction* for hand-crafted ops: an op map that is unmistakably
"a citadel city on a river during Tet" without the nameplate (see §3). That's still B.
The line to hold: **the moment a player-playable space carries a real name, we've moved
to C** and inherit C's costs. Hold at B everywhere the player shoots.

## 2 · What Tet MEANS dramatically — and how a no-cutscene game says it

Tet's drama is a five-act structure that maps perfectly onto a firebase sim's ambient
channels. The player is never TOLD any of this; he assembles it, which is the whole
open-sim creed ("leave the camp and go find problems").

**Act I — the quiet (late January).** The historical tell: contact DROPPED before Tet.
Patrols found cold trails. Villages emptied of young men. In-game: the ambient encounter
director eases off — fewer contacts, colder camps, and the player who's learned the AO's
normal rhythm FEELS the wrongness. A game that has taught baseline can weaponize silence.
Writable artifacts: patrol overheards ("Three days, not one shot. I hate it."); an intel
overheard at the TOC tent ("S-2 says infiltration's up but contact's down, make it make
sense"); villagers stockpiling rice; a barber in the vill who stops showing up.

**Act II — the truce.** The ceasefire announced for the holiday. Radio traffic relaxes;
a stand-down order comes over the net; someone strings a hammock. Stars & Stripes issue
on the chow-hall table: "ALLIES ANNOUNCE TET CEASEFIRE." The chaplain schedules a
service. Firecrackers audible from the vill at dusk — indistinguishable, at first, from
small arms. (That's a real detail and a gift to a sound-driven game: the player's
threat-parsing ear is deliberately jammed by the holiday itself.)

**Act III — the night it breaks (30–31 Jan).** Not a cutscene — a RADIO EVENT. The net,
which has been sleepy, goes vertical: flash traffic stepping on flash traffic, other
callsigns' firefights bleeding through, the battalion net asking questions nobody
answers. Illum rounds on the horizon in three directions. Then our own wire probe /
mortar stonk (the siege system already built). The player experiences Tet the way every
real firebase did: as EVERYONE ELSE'S emergency arriving over the radio minutes before
his own arrives over the wire.

**Act IV — the long February.** Hue burns offstage for 26 days. The war's texture
changes: medevac priority is suddenly hard to get ("all birds committed north"), resupply
gets thin, artillery has competing fire missions, refugee columns walk Highway 1 past
the wire — old men, hooches' worth of belongings, no military-age males. ARVN allies'
mood (see artifacts below). The ops tempo rises: this is where the Summoner's helicopter
operations live, dramatically — the firebase gets REACHED INTO by the offensive and men
are lifted out to other people's fights.

**Act V — the hollow after.** The offensive fails militarily and wins anyway. Stars &
Stripes claims victory in the same week the short-timers stop believing anything. New
overheard register: "We won? Then why's everything still on fire?" This act is the tonal
north star (Platoon's exhausted end) and costs only writing.

### Diegetic artifact inventory (all writable now, all cheap, none need cutscenes)
1. **The radio net** — the master instrument. Escalation is measured in traffic density,
   procedure discipline decaying under load, and OTHER units' wars overheard. (§4.)
2. **Stars & Stripes** — a prop newspaper on the chow-hall table, new issue every few
   game-days; headline + two subheads is all we render. The paper is cheerful and wrong
   in period-accurate ways; the gap between the headline and the player's week IS the
   Vietnam tone. ~15 issues covers a tour arc.
3. **Overheard barks by campaign phase** — the existing garrison schedule gains a
   phase-keyed bark table (quiet / truce / offensive / grind / after). Biggest tone
   return per line of content in the whole plan.
4. **ARVN presence** — an ARVN liaison element at the firebase (faction stature decree
   already gives us the roster logic). Pre-Tet: card games, trading, an interpreter.
   During: half of them gone — Tet leave, exactly as happened — and the ones left are
   frantic; their home cities are the ones burning. The single most humanizing channel
   we have and almost free.
5. **Refugee columns** — a scripted ambient: civilians on the road past the wire,
   walking south. No interaction required to land; the litter-team/civilian pipeline
   already gives us bodies and walk cycles. (Atrocity-hook adjacency noted; keep
   refugees a SIGHT, not a system, for now.)
6. **The chaplain's service** — Tet-eve service under the truce (Act II warmth), and a
   memorial service with the boots-rifle-helmet after the siege (Act IV weight). Two
   staged scenes, existing splice pipeline.
7. **Mail call / short-timer calendar** — already-native firebase texture that gains
   meaning when the offensive interrupts it ("mail bird got retasked").

## 3 · The Hue-adjacent city — name it or not?

**My call: do not name it Hue. Build "the citadel city" and let the player's radio call
it by OUR name.** Reasons:
- Hue owns two things we must not gamify on the nameplate: 26 days of block-by-block
  Marine casualties, and the massacre of ~2,800 civilians by the occupiers. A fictional
  citadel city lets us USE the second (discovered ditches, an interrogation overheard,
  the civilian cost) as fiction, with the moral seriousness of fiction, instead of as a
  claim about real named dead.
- At PSX scale we cannot survive the comparison to the real geography, and the people
  who love this period are exactly the people who will measure.
- Naming it also collapses the seeded-tour fiction at the campaign level: if the city is
  Hue, the firebase must be in Thua Thien, and every tour is pinned to one province.

**What the city reads as (the kit list — every item screams "imperial city on a river"):**
- **The Citadel**: massive brick curtain walls with earthen fill, corner bastions,
  arched gates with gate-towers, a moat. PSX loves this — huge simple volumes, one
  brick sheet. Walls are the op map's spine: fighting INTO a gate is the set-piece.
- **The Perfume-analog river** + canal grid: sampans, a destroyed steel-truss bridge
  (half-spans in the water — instantly "Tet"), monsoon-grey water (water is geometry,
  per the 8/12 decree).
- **Pagodas and a royal enclosure**: tiered roofs, dragon finials, courtyard walls —
  verticality for snipers, courtyards for our proven modular-building fighting.
- **French colonial quarter** south of the river: shuttered two-storey stucco blocks,
  balconies, a church with a shell hole, a lycée, a hospital with a red cross on the
  roof. The Hue battle was really two battles (Citadel north, colonial town south) and
  that structure is a gift: two hand-crafted op maps from one city identity.
- **The flag moment, ours**: the enemy flag flying over the citadel gate — raising ours
  is the op's visible end-state. Fictional flag, real image. (The casualty ledger stays
  the scoreboard; the flag is just the picture.)
- Name direction: give it a Vietnamese name with the same music — two syllables, river
  royalty — e.g. **Phu Vang**, **Kim Giang**, **An Xuan** (check maps so we don't
  accidentally hit a real district that had its own history; An Cuu and Phu Bai are
  real, avoid). Radio callsign for the city fight writes itself: "the Citadel."

## 4 · Radio/RTO assignment language — how ops arrive (never a screen)

Register rules first, because they ARE the authenticity: prowords (this-is, over, out,
copy, how-copy, actual, interrogative), phonetic alphabet, callsigns as identity, map
coordinates as ritual ("from my pos, up two, right four" only informally — formal traffic
uses grid), no exposition — real traffic assumes shared context, and the PLAYER filling
the gaps is the immersion engine. Escalation is written in PROCEDURE DECAY: early nets
are crisp; under Tet load, procedure frays, voices step on each other, and that fraying
is audible drama no cutscene can buy.

**Sample 1 — the warning order (a day before an op; texture, not tasking):**
> "Bravo Six, Bravo Six, this is Ripcord Three. Warning order follows, break. Be advised,
> your element is placed on strip alert effective 0600 tomorrow. Anticipate lift
> assets. Nature of tasking to follow. How copy, over."
> "Ripcord Three, Bravo Six. Good copy. Any word on where, over."
> "Bravo Six, Ripcord Three. You'll know when I know. Ripcord Three, out."

**Sample 2 — the helicopter assault tasking (op launch; the player just hears his RTO):**
> "Bravo Six, this is Ripcord Three, flash traffic, flash traffic. Stand your people to
> and move to the pad, time now. Lift is six Uniform Hotel Ones, ETA one-five mikes.
> You are going into LZ Crow, grid to follow on the bird. Snake escort is up.
> Expect a hot LZ, say again, expect hot. Acknowledge, over."
> RTO, off-net, to the squad: "Saddle up! Birds inbound, we're going north!"

**Sample 3 — hold-the-LZ (the ~20-min hold op):**
> "All stations this net, this is Pedestal Six Actual. Crow is ours as of time now.
> Nobody comes off this perimeter. Chalks two through six are twenty mikes out with
> the guns and the wire. We keep this dirt or they land in the trees. Pedestal Six,
> out."

**Sample 4 — the Tet break (Act III radio event; overheard, not addressed to you):**
> "—any station, any station, this net, Mustang Five, we are in contact, multiple—"
> "—station calling, you are stepping on flash traffic—"
> "—Panther X-ray, Panther X-ray, tower's gone, they're inside the wire at the province
> compound, fire mission, danger close, authenticate whiskey-tango—"
> Then, to you, finally: "Bravo Six, Ripcord Three. Stand-down is cancelled. Get your
> people on the wire. It's everywhere. Over."

**Sample 5 — the citadel op assignment (city fight; note real-war borrowing of tone,
no real names):**
> "Bravo Six, Ripcord Three. Tasking follows, break. Element of NVA regimental strength
> holds the old city at Kim Giang, break. ARVN First Division holds the north corner —
> that's all they hold, break. You will lift at first light and marry up with Task
> Force Corsair south of the river. Bring everything. This one's house to house.
> How copy, over."

Craft note: each op type needs only ~3 exchange-templates (warning order / launch /
en-route color) with slot-in nouns — the seeded campaign can reskin callsigns and LZ
names per tour, which keeps the radio inside the seeded-tour premise too.

## 5 · 1968-flavor combat content worth adding (period-correct Tet menu)

In rough order of dramatic value per unit of new work:
1. **Sappers in cities** — already have the sapper breach chain; recontexting it to a
   city block (satchel through a compound wall) is the signature Tet image. Cheap, ours.
2. **B-40/B-41 volleys** (RPG-2/7 already in armory) fired in salvo at buildings, not
   armor — the Tet urban signature sound. Mostly encounter-director scripting.
3. **57mm/75mm recoilless rifles** — the crew-served bunker-buster of the offensive;
   a crewed weapon with a backblast is a readable, killable, period-perfect set-piece
   emplacement for city and siege maps. One weapon system, big flavor.
4. **122mm rocket attacks on the firebase** — Tet's calling card against bases; a
   longer, screaming incoming distinct from mortars. Audio + the existing siege stonk
   logic. (The whoosh-scream is free dread every night thereafter.)
5. **PT-76s — yes, if we dare, and only as an OP, never ambient.** Lang Vei is the
   period license: light amphibious tanks at night, overrunning a camp. As a single
   hand-crafted operation ("armor in the wire" — the one night the war breaks its own
   rules), it is the strongest possible use: the player's whole tour has taught him the
   enemy has no armor, and then the radio says "tanks" and nobody believes it —
   EXACTLY as happened. That disbelief is writable ("Say again your last. Did you say
   tracks?"). LAW already does 250 damage; the M72 finally earns its slot. Cost is a
   vehicle + crude armor AI; scope it as one scripted op, not a system.
6. **Firecrackers/holiday ambience as threat-jamming** (Act II) — audio only, huge.
7. **CS gas / gas masks in the city** — period-used at Hue; probably a system too far;
   mention for completeness, recommend cut.
8. **ARVN allies fighting alongside** (M1 carbines/Garands already period, faction
   stature covers stature) — one op where the player reinforces an ARVN-held sector
   pays off the humanizing channel from §2.

## 6 · Tradeoffs named (the law)

- **Option B sacrifices** deniability: dating the game to Jan–Mar 1968 means period
  errors become bugs (wrong rifle variant, wrong slang, an anachronistic song). We
  accept a standing authenticity duty. It also spends the 7/28 decree's letter —
  requires a knowing amendment (dates real, ground fictional).
- **Not naming Hue sacrifices** marketing recognition — "fight the Battle of Hue" is a
  stronger store-page line than "a citadel city burns." I judge the trade correct;
  the store page can still say *set during the Tet Offensive*, which carries the
  recognition without the claims.
- **The Tet season arc sacrifices** some pure-sandbox neutrality: a campaign with a
  five-act weather system is less "any tour, any shape" than the open-sim ideal. The
  seed still owns the ground and the stories; the calendar becomes shared. I'd argue
  the shared calendar is a feature (community talks to each other across different
  maps: "where were you when it broke?"), but it IS a narrowing, and the Arbiter
  should weigh it.
- **PT-76 op sacrifices** the fear doctrine's purity (infantry war) for one night; the
  fence is "one scripted op, never ambient armor," and that fence must be written in
  the decree or it will creep.

## 7 · Recommendation

**Option B + C+ fold-in: real offensive, real calendar, real war on the radio; fictional
firebase, fictional battalion, fictional citadel city — every space the player shoots in
is ours.** Amend the 7/28 decree explicitly: "fictional GROUND and dead" replaces
"fictional dates." Build the Tet five-act arc as the campaign's ambient season (radio
density, bark tables, Stars & Stripes, ARVN mood, refugees), deliver ops through RTO
traffic per §4 templates, and hold the city at "Hue-adjacent, never Hue-named." Period
content priority: sappers-in-city, RPG volleys, recoilless rifles, 122mm rockets, the
one PT-76 night.
