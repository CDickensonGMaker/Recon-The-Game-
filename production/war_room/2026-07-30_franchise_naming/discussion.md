# The Debate

**Agreement, arrived at independently:** all three Architects converge on the same conclusion by different roads — Game Designer via pillar-signal mismatch, Writer via voice/identity, Devil's Advocate via legal exposure. None of them needed the others' argument to get there, which is a strong signal, not a coincidence of groupthink.

**Where Writer and Game Designer align:** both want to keep "Hell" as the emotional/tonal anchor — it's era-appropriate across all three planned settings (Vietnam, Korea, WWI all have "hell" as earned vocabulary in their respective war literatures) and it's the half of the original pun that was never borrowed from Call of Duty in the first place.

**Where Devil's Advocate adds a constraint the other two didn't weigh:** even if tone were a non-issue, the trademark risk alone is sufficient to kill "Hell of Duty" as a shippable name, and that risk gets *worse*, not better, the more successful the franchise plan becomes. This reframes the decision from "which name sounds best" to "which risks are we willing to compound across three future titles."

**Tension surfaced:** Writer's instinct is to solve this now with an alternate name; Devil's Advocate pushes back that naming under momentum — mid-conversation, without a trademark screen — is exactly how a second bad name gets adopted. Game Designer sides with Devil's Advocate here: better to bank the *structural* decision (umbrella + era tag, "Hell" as root) and let the specific base name get proposed and vetted in its own pass.

**Standing decree conflict:** all three note the 7/28 "name stays RECON" ruling isn't being violated by this discussion — no new name is being adopted today — but flag that if/when a replacement is chosen, it must be recorded as an explicit supersession, not left to quietly coexist with RECON in the docs.
