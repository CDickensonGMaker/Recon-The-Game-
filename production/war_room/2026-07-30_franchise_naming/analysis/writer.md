# Writer — Individual Sight

The pun is memorable, I'll give it that — it's the kind of title that gets repeated in a Discord or a Steam comment thread, which has real marketing value. And the umbrella + subtitle pattern (base name, era tag per title) is a proven franchise shape — it's literally how Call of Duty itself scales, and how Battlefield, Close Combat, and Company of Heroes all handle sub-theatres.

But borrowing that exact shape from the franchise we're positioning against is a voice problem, not just a legal one (Devil's Advocate will carry the legal weight). A pun built entirely out of a rival's name means the name's entire personality is "not-quite-Call-of-Duty" — it never gets to be its own thing, which directly undercuts what Caleb said drove this conversation in the first place: *"it truly is turning into something of its own."* The name should catch up to that, not lag behind it.

"Hell" as a root word is worth keeping — it's tonally earned by the genre (Vietnam War narrative leans on damnation/hell imagery constantly) and it travels well across eras too: hell fits WWI trench warfare and Korea's "Frozen Chosin" both, without forcing a rewrite of the umbrella brand per title. That's a real asset — don't lose it chasing a completely different direction.

**Recommendation:** preserve "Hell" as the emotional anchor of the umbrella brand, drop "of Duty" as the specific construction, and let the era tag do what it's already doing well (Vietnam / Korea / WWI). Treat "Hell of Duty" as the internal joke that got the idea moving — fine to keep saying it around the table, wrong to put it on a store page.
