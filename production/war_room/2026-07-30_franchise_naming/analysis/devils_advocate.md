# Devil's Advocate — Individual Sight

Two hard objections, independent of the tone argument the other two are making.

**1. Trademark exposure, and it compounds with franchise scale.** *Call of Duty* is one of the most aggressively defended trademarks in the entertainment industry (Activision, now Microsoft). A single indie title with a punning name might slide under the radar for a while, or it might not — but Caleb has explicitly said the plan is to reuse this as a **franchise umbrella** across Vietnam, Korea, and WWI titles. That means the legal exposure isn't a one-time roll of the dice on one game, it's baked into every future release under the same brand. If a C&D or UDRP-style challenge ever lands, it doesn't just cost one title's storefront listing — it costs the *entire franchise identity* built on top of it, mid-build. That is the single worst time to discover a naming problem.

**2. This session would silently override a standing decree.** The 2026-07-28 operations decree ruled the project name **stays RECON**. Nothing wrong with revisiting that — the Summoner holds final authority and can reopen any prior ruling — but Law 1 requires it be done explicitly, not by drift. If "Hell of Duty: Vietnam" (or any successor name) is adopted, that has to be recorded as an explicit supersession of the 7/28 decree, not left ambiguous with two "current" project names floating in the docs.

**What's sacrificed either way:**
- If we ship the pun: we sacrifice legal safety and the "this is its own thing" identity Caleb wants, in exchange for a catchy, easy-to-repeat name.
- If we drop it entirely and keep RECON: we sacrifice the marketing hook of a memorable pun and the franchise-tag structure stays theoretical until a real replacement base name exists.

**Recommendation:** treat "Hell of Duty" as unshippable as a literal title — even as a coincidence-of-wording, the risk profile is bad enough to fail this Council on its own merits before tone is even considered. Don't adopt a replacement name in this same session either — naming-under-time-pressure is how bad brand decisions happen. Task a follow-up brainstorm pass, and don't treat any candidate as final until it's been run through even an informal trademark screen (USPTO TESS / TMview) before Caleb gets attached to it.
