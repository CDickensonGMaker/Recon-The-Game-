# Game Designer — Individual Sight

A game's name is the first pillar-test a player ever runs, before they've touched a single system. "Hell of Duty" reads, unavoidably, as a Call of Duty joke — and Call of Duty signals killstreaks, scripted setpieces, forgiving TTK, rails. That's close to the exact opposite of what RECON is:

- **Pillar 1 (outstanding gunplay, HLL lethality, no bullet sponges)** — a CoD-adjacent name primes players to expect arcade lethality. First impression works against the pitch instead of for it.
- **Pillar 3 (freedom, no rails, ever)** — CoD is the most rail-heavy AAA shooter franchise that exists. Naming ourselves after it, even as a pun, invites the exact comparison our design explicitly rejects.
- **Pillar 5 (fail forward, permadeath squad, no reload-and-memorize)** — nothing about "Duty" signals persistent consequence. Hell Let Loose's own name does more of that work (attrition, no glory).

The "Hell" half is genuinely good — visceral, era-appropriate (Vietnam War fiction/film leans on exactly that word: *Platoon*, *Apocalypse Now* iconography, "hell" is baked into the genre's vocabulary already). It's the "of Duty" half that's fighting the pitch, not helping it.

**Franchise-umbrella structure itself is sound design** — base name + era tag scales cleanly to Korea/WWI, and it matches how the TerrainEngine is already architected (region-swappable reference data + presets). I'd keep that *structure*. I would not keep the *specific pun* it launched from.

**Recommendation:** keep the umbrella pattern, replace the CoD echo with a base name that signals hardcore/tactical/attritional identity on its own — something a genre-savvy player reads and immediately recodes their expectations toward HLL/Arma, not CoD.
