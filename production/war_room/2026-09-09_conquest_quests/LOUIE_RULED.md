# LOUIE, RULED — and the language seam, checked against the pages
**2026-09-09.** Companion to `synthesis.md` and `THE_MERCY_AND_CORRECTIONS.md` (this folder).
**Post-demo-launch. Nothing built.**

> **HIS RULING, verbatim: "hes a lousiana man that can speak french and went to fight."**

An American — a Louisiana French speaker — in French service. **He is in someone else's army because he
already had the language.** Not a random volunteer.

---

# 1 · WHAT THIS SUPERSEDES, AND THE ONE WRINKLE IT LEAVES

**Superseded.** `CONQUEST_OF_WORMS_BIBLE.md` §2 currently reads *"**French**, from Aix-en-Provence."*
That is now wrong and the bible needs a correction note. **I have not edited it** — it is his untracked
draft. Recorded here so it is not read as canon tomorrow.

**The wrinkle, stated rather than smoothed.** The pages put Louie in **Aix-en-Provence in April 1915**,
leaving for Paris (I2 p6), and his **mother the medium is at the parting** (I2 p7; I3 p3, *"Inside you
are two dogs, one red and one black"*). If he is a Louisiana man, why is his mother seeing him off in
Provence?

**The cheapest reconciliation, and it costs nothing — offered as a proposal:**
**The family is Louisiana French with Provence roots, and Louie went back to the old country and
enlisted from there.** That makes his own synopsis line literal (*"travels oversaes and joins the french
army"*), keeps every drawn Aix page valid, and explains the language, the name and the medium mother in
one stroke.

**And the pages leave the door wide open for it.** Checked: **the four issues never give Louie a
surname.** The bible records him only as *"LOUIE (LOUIS) — Michael's grandfather."* Michael is
**Crawford** — a Scots-English name. So **nothing forces Louie to be a Crawford.** Make him the
**maternal** grandfather and the French line, the medium mother and the Louisiana French all sit
consistent with zero retconning. **His to bless or throw out.**

**One thing to check before drawing, because it is against type.** Michael's draft card says **Minden,
Louisiana** — that is **Webster Parish, northwest Louisiana**, historically Anglo-Protestant settlement,
not French Acadiana, which is the south of the state. A French-speaking Louisiana family is far more
usual in the south. Not impossible in the north, and Louie need not be from Minden at all — Michael was
born there in 1950, two generations later. **But if he wants the French to be unremarkable, the family
came up from south Louisiana, and that is worth deciding before a face is drawn.**

# 2 · A RHYME THE PAGES DO SUPPORT — **LOUIS, NAMED FOR A KING, FROM LOUISIANA**

This one is on the page and it got stronger under his ruling, not weaker. Issue 2 p7, his mother:
> *"**Louis, even kings face their share of challenges.** But with that determination of yours, who
> knows? Maybe you'll rewrite history in your own way."*

The bible records the beat as *"Named for a king."* **Louisiana is named for the same king** — Louis XIV.
So a Louisiana boy called Louis is named for the man his home state is named for, and he crosses an
ocean to fight for that king's country.

**Nothing has to be written for this. It is already in his dialogue and already on the draft card.**
Load-bearing? No — texture. But it is free, it is his, and it is the kind of detail that rewards a
second reading.

# 3 · THE LANGUAGE SEAM — **PROPOSED, and here is exactly how much the pages carry**

The proposal: Vietnam was French Indochina; the elders Michael walks past speak the language his
grandfather went to war in; Michael is deaf in it. One generation had the tongue and crossed an ocean to
use it. The next was carried into the same empire's leftovers and cannot understand a word.

**Checked against the pages. The verdict is: the ends are drawn, the middle is not.**

| Link | On the page? |
|---|---|
| Michael is from **Minden, Louisiana** | **YES** — drawn on the Selective Service card, I1 title page |
| Louie fights in **French service** | **YES** — poilu kit, Lt. Durand, Notre-Dame de Lorette, I2 pp.6-16 |
| Louie is a Louisiana French speaker | **RULING, 2026-09-09.** Not on any page |
| **A Vietnamese character speaks French** | **NO.** The market vendor (I3 p8) is *"the only Vietnamese character in the four issues who speaks"*, and her line is in **English**: a ghost *"out looking for young American Soldier"* |
| **Indochina / the Legion appears** | **NO PAGE.** The bully's Foreign Legion career in Indochina is in his synopsis only, and the bible's causal table marks it *"NO PAGE"* |

**So: do not treat the seam as canon.** It is historically exact — French was the administrative and
elite language of Indochina and older Vietnamese absolutely spoke it — and it requires no invention of
fact. But **it invents a scene type the comic does not have**, and it puts words in the mouth of the one
Vietnamese demographic the book has so far only drawn as terrain. **That cuts both ways and he should
know it does.** It is the single cheapest way to give a Vietnamese character something to say that is
not about Americans — which is the gap the treatment already flagged as his to close.

**If he blesses it, the smallest honest version — and it is a QUEST, not a cutscene:**
- An **old villager who answers in French.** Michael does not understand him. **No subtitle.** The
  player hears a language and gets nothing, which is the whole point.
- The journal is the only French in the game the player CAN read, because it is rendered as text in
  Louie's cursive face. **So the player can read a language the character cannot hear.**
- **The play:** the player may recognise a word from the journal in what the old man says. Nothing marks
  it. Nothing checks off. If he catches it, he can bring it back to the page and it means something; if
  he doesn't, an old man said something and a young man pointed a rifle.
- **What it costs:** the moment the game subtitles the old man, this is dead. The affordance must be
  the journal in the player's own hands, never a translation.
- **The theme arriving as play, not pageantry:** the player is doing the comprehending, or failing to.
  The grandfather could have talked to this man. The grandson cannot. **Nobody in the game says that.**

# 4 · THE MERCY, STAGED UNDER THE RULING

Unchanged in mechanism (see `THE_MERCY_AND_CORRECTIONS.md` Part A): **he gets away regardless, there is
never a body, and Louie does not know either.** What the ruling changes is the man holding the rifle.

**Art:** American face, Louisiana man, **in poilu kit** — Adrian helmet, horizon-blue, **Lebel or
Berthier** (bolt-action; one shot before the German is out of it, which is what makes the fixed outcome
honest). **Not a doughboy. Not a Frenchman.** Seventeen in April 1915.

**And the ruling sharpens the parallel the synopsis already names.** One generation **had the language
and crossed an ocean to get into a war.** The next was **carried into one** — the draft card is the page
the story is printed on. His words: *"One generation crossed an ocean to get into a war. The next was
carried into one."* Under this ruling both men are Louisiana men, which makes it a family fact rather
than a coincidence of nationality.

# 5 · OWED — he has NOT ruled this and I am not assuming it

**Is THE MERCY a full cutscene, or a cutscene with the trigger live in the player's hands?**
The two are different games and the difference is the whole point of the beat.

- **Trigger live.** The player's own finger. Costs: an authored sequence that must survive a player who
  shoots the ground, shoots Durand, walks away, or does nothing until it times out — all four need an
  answer, and "nothing happens" must be one of the two records. **This is the version the synopsis
  argues for:** *"If he cannot, the mercy is scenery."*
- **Full cutscene.** Cheap, reliable, and it makes the journal entry an authored fact rather than the
  player's. **Then there is only one record and Part A's two-record payload is gone.**

**These are not close in cost and they are not close in meaning. His call.**
