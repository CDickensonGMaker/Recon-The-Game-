# GAME DESIGNER — THE ROADS NETWORK
**Council:** 2026-07-20 roads_network · **Charge:** Q2 (primary), Q3, Q5 · **Lens:** the Five Pillars, Rule #1

---

## VERDICT IN ONE LINE

**A road is not a rail — but the road we are tempted to build IS one, and the code proves it.**
The line between "feature of the ground" and "rail" is not drawn by geometry. It is drawn by
**whether the ground beside the road is a real option**, and by **which direction the road's
gradient points**. Six rules below hold it on the right side. One of them —
**THE NO-SPEED RULE** — is non-negotiable and I want it in the decree verbatim.

---

## Q2 · IS A ROAD NETWORK A RAIL?

### 1 · The definition Pillar 3 actually means

Pillar 3 is not "no lines on the ground." Rivers are lines. Ridgelines are lines. The wire is a
line. Pillar 3 forbids **corridors that are the only viable way**, and ADR-020 already gave us the
binding test in a different context:

> *Can he leave, right now, unpunished?*

Transpose it: **can he step off the road, right now, and still get where he was going?**
If yes, the road is terrain. If no, the road is a rail wearing a texture.

That is the whole line. Everything else in this analysis is machinery to keep the answer YES.

### 2 · The inversion risk is REAL, and it is already latent in the code

The briefing frames the tradeoff as *"fast + exposed + mined vs slow + concealed + lost."*
**That tradeoff does not exist in this build.** Measured:

- `scripts/player/player.gd:841-842` — the player's speed is divided by a terrain cost **for
  `RICE_PADDY` and nothing else.** Heavy jungle costs the player **zero** movement speed.
- `terrain/core/gameplay_grid.gd:27-36` — `MOVEMENT_COSTS` exists with real jungle numbers
  (LIGHT 1.3 / MEDIUM 1.6 / HEAVY 2.2), and outside the paddy line it is read by exactly one
  consumer: `scripts/enemies/patrol_generator.gd:74`. **It is an AI-pathing table, not a player
  contract.**

So the "slow" half of "slow + concealed" is fiction. If we ship a road that grants speed, we have
not created a choice — we have created a **strictly dominant traversal surface**, because it costs
the player nothing to leave the jungle he was never being slowed by. That is the inversion the
briefing fears, and it arrives by default unless we forbid it.

The naive fix — "add jungle slowdown so the road has something to beat" — is a **Pillar 1 change
smuggled in as a roads feature.** It re-tunes every firefight's ability to reposition, and it is a
separate decree. Do not let the roads wave touch it.

### 3 · THE CORRECTION: the road's gift is LEGIBILITY, not VELOCITY

Here is what roads actually gave a grunt, and it was never speed — a column on a laterite road in
1967 moved at the pace of its point man and its mine-sweep team, which is **slower than the same
men in light bush**. What the road gave was **certainty**: you know where you are, you know where
this comes out, you will not walk in a circle for two hours, and you can move a truck on it.

And **getting lost is a live, real cost in this game.** There is no minimap; ADR-022 gives the
player a topo sheet and a grease pencil and explicitly permits him to be *wrong*. Navigational
certainty is therefore a genuine currency here, not a flavour note.

> ## **THE ROAD PAYS IN ORIENTATION AND CHARGES IN EXPOSURE.**
> It never pays in speed. It never pays in time. It pays only in *knowing where you are*.

This single reframe keeps the Vietnam truth upright. The road is the **dangerous** ground; its lure
is that it is **easy to follow**, not that it is quick to walk. Men took it anyway — because they
were tired, because they were moving a vehicle, because navigating 4 km of triple canopy on a
compass is genuinely hard. That is a *human* temptation, not a *mechanical optimum*, and it is
exactly the shape of temptation this game should trade in.

### 4 · The other half: a road is not a path the DESIGNER drew

The briefing's sharpest phrasing is *"a road is literally a path the designer drew across the AO."*
It only stays true if we let it. **A road generated by connecting sites that already exist is
derived from the world, not imposed on it.** The firebase needs resupply; villages pay taxes and
sell rice; the engineers cut a track between them. That is a road the *fiction* drew.

The failure mode is the reverse: a road laid down first, and then villages nudged to sit on it.
That is level design, and it will produce exactly the funnel Pillar 3 forbids. Hence Rule 3 below.

### 5 · THE SIX RULES (the decree text I am asking for)

| # | Rule | Why | Testable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **THE ADJACENCY RULE.** Every metre of road must have walkable ground within ~50 m on at least one side that reaches the same destination. No road segment may ever be the only passable route between two points. | This IS Pillar 3. A road you cannot leave is a corridor. | Yes — headless: sample road polyline, assert a parallel walkable corridor exists (reuse `PatrolGenerator._is_walkable`). |
| **2** | **THE NO-SPEED RULE.** A road never moves the player faster than the open ground beside it. `MOVEMENT_COSTS[ROAD]` may not be lower than `CLEAR` (1.0), and the paddy divisor at `player.gd:842` is not extended to grant a road bonus. | Prevents the inversion. Without this rule the road is dominant on day one. | Yes — probe the constant; probe that the player-speed path has no road branch. |
| **3** | **THE DERIVED RULE.** Roads may only connect sites that already exist in `plan_patrol_world`. A road may never cause a site to exist, and no site may be moved to fit a road. Roads are computed AFTER site placement, and site placement never reads the road graph. | Keeps roads a consequence of the world instead of a skeleton for it. Also protects ADR-028's one-build-path. | Yes — ordering assertion in the build probe. |
| **4** | **THE LOUD RULE.** Road ground is hard, dry and open: footstep noise radius up (the `quiet_mult` lever already exists at `player.gd:661-670`), `COVER_VALUES[ROAD] = 0.0`, no clutter, no concealment. | The road's cost must be *felt in the stealth economy*, which is where this game's real currency lives (ADR-006). | Yes — noise-radius probe. |
| **5** | **THE THREAT RULE.** The road must be the most dangerous ground in the AO and must remain so. VC ambush siting *prefers* it; later waves put mines on it. **If a playtest shows the player walking roads by default, the design has failed — and the fix is more threat, never a movement penalty.** | Names the failure condition in advance so nobody "fixes" it by nerfing walking. | By playtest only (ADR-015). |
| **6** | **THE NO-GATE RULE.** Nothing may require the road. No location, no pointer, no site entrance, no wire-gate bearing, no bridge-as-sole-crossing. The FieldDirector pointer may never route *along* a road. | The rail always sneaks in as a *requirement*, never as a road. | Yes — assert the pointer emits a bearing, not a path. |

**Rules 1, 2 and 6 are the pillar-load-bearing three.** Rules 3, 4, 5 are what make the road
*good*; 1/2/6 are what make it *legal*.

### 6 · Where I'd concede the road IS a rail

Honest edge case: **bridges.** A bridge over an uncrossable river is, by construction, a violation
of Rule 1 — it is the only way across, and everyone funnels to it. That is a genuine rail, and it
is also the single most Vietnam-shaped ambush object we could build. My ruling: **cut bridges from
wave 1** (see Q5). If they ever come back, they come back only where a **ford exists within 300 m**,
so the bridge is a convenience the player may decline. A bridge with a ford is terrain. A bridge
without one is a level-design chokepoint, and the *player* must never be routed through it.

Second edge case: the **convoy** is railed by the road, and that is correct and diegetic. Vehicles
are supposed to be prisoners of the road — that is *why* the ambush works. Pillar 3 protects the
player, not the trucks.

## Q3 · DOES THE AMBUSH ECONOMY IMPROVE? (weighing in)

### The rotation problem is real, and the road is not the thing that rotates

ADR-021 §2 is explicit: **a successful ambush costs you your intel.** A road is permanent; it
cannot rotate; a topo sheet prints it from the start. If the intel is *"there is a road here,"* the
player learns it in five seconds and it never expires — the economy collapses.

**But that is a category error about what the intel IS.**

> The burnable intel was never *"there is a road here."*
> It is *"a resupply run comes down this stretch around dusk, two trucks and a gun jeep."*

The road is the **stage**. The **traffic** is the intel, and traffic rotates natively — reroute,
re-time, re-escort, or stop running that leg entirely. This is historically exact (a convoy hit
once got escort, air cover, a sweep team, or a different road), it slots into ADR-021 without
amending a word, and it makes the road *strengthen* the loop instead of breaking it.

**Anti-farm, stated as a rule:** hit a stretch and traffic on that stretch **drops** — fewer runs,
heavier escort, or the route moves. The kill zone self-extinguishes diegetically. No cooldown
timer, no invisible hand.

Note also that ADR-006 already blunts the farming worry from the other side: **kills pay zero.** A
farmable road kill zone pays no XP by construction. The farm risk here is not economic, it is
*experiential* — an infinite target that makes the AO feel like a shooting gallery. The traffic-
drop rule is what handles that.

### ROAD_NEAR_M must be a SCORE, never a GATE

The briefing's warning at Q3 is correct and I want to underline it hard. `ambush_planner.plan()`
(`scripts/enemies/ambush_planner.gd:30-63`) currently `continue`s on paddy proximity, cover floor
and keepout, and returns the best-scored survivor. **If we add a hard `continue` on road distance,
every camp with no road inside 80 m returns `{}` — and camps are placed with no knowledge of roads
(Rule 3 guarantees that). We would ship a planner that plans nothing.** That is strictly worse than
today's live-but-undocumented behaviour.

Correct shape: fold road proximity into the existing weighted score at `:54`, alongside
`cover_score` and `los_score`. The planner then *prefers* the road when there is one and still
sites a trail ambush when there isn't. That satisfies the truth-law complaint (the constant becomes
read, and the header becomes true when reworded from "requirement" to "preference") without making
the system worse.

### The substrate is neither "road" nor "circuit" — it is LINEAR TRAFFIC

Bead ld0y offers roads *or* PatrolGenerator circuits. **The answer is that both are instances of
one concept**, and naming it that way is the design win:

> **A good ambush needs a line people move along, predictably.** A road, a trail, a patrol circuit,
> a river ford, a paddy dike. `ROAD_NEAR_M` is misnamed; the design constant is
> **TRAFFIC_NEAR_M** — distance to the nearest *predictable movement corridor*, whatever its type.

That single rename resolves Q3's dilemma, makes the constant honest, and means we are not "solving
the constant instead of the design." It also generalises to VC ambushing the *player*, which is the
inversion done right: **the AI should prefer to ambush you on the road**, because that is where the
war was.

## RULE #1 · WHAT DOES A ROAD ADD TO WALKING THE AO?

**Straight answer: the road itself is inert. The LIFE on it is the feature.**

An empty dirt track is a texture. But I do not think it is *worthless* — a road delivers three
things a jungle-only AO cannot:

1. **A spine for the walk.** It gives the AO a readable structure — a thing to cross, a thing to
   parallel, a thing to lie up and watch. **"Move parallel to the road, watching it"** is a real
   Vietnam verb this game currently has no way to express. That verb alone is worth the wave.
2. **A place where the player's decision is visible to himself.** Crossing an open road is a
   discrete, tense, sixty-second problem. The game has very few of those right now.
3. **A reason for a firebase to be where it is.** Roads make the AO *make sense*, which is Pillar 2.

But if wave 1 ships a road with zero traffic, **we shipped scenery**, and I want that expectation
set now rather than discovered at playtest. The feature is not "there are roads in the AO." The
feature is **"there is traffic in this AO."**

Priority order for that life, by fun-per-unit-of-work:
1. **Convoys** — already built (`scripts/vehicles/convoy.gd`), routeless, and the one wired dynamic-
   event producer in the game. Best ratio in the project right now.
2. **Civilians on foot / ox carts** — cheap, and they turn the road into an *ROE* problem, which
   feeds ADR-019 directly. A cart on the road is a fire-discipline test with no combat cost.
3. **VC using the road at night**, or crossing it — the ambush target that *rotates*.
4. **Refugee columns** — highest atmosphere, highest content cost. Later.

## Q5 · SCOPE — WHAT I WOULD CUT

**KEEP (wave 1, and this is the whole wave):**
- One road authority owning a **polyline graph**, generated after site placement, connecting
  `fsb_main` gate → villages → camps-adjacent (never *into* a camp).
- A **route array** the existing `Convoy.setup()` can consume unchanged.
- **TRAFFIC_NEAR_M as a soft score term** in `ambush_planner`.
- **Printed on the topo sheet from mission start** (see Q4 note below).
- **One** convoy, running **one** route, on a schedule.

**CUT — and I will defend each:**

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| **Bridges** | Violates the Adjacency Rule by construction (see Q2 §6), and drags in river/hydrology risk that was *just* stabilised. A road that stops at a river and resumes on the far bank is a real object — that is a **ford**, and it is free. |
| **Junctions as a system** | Let polylines cross. No junction node type, no intersection logic, no checkpoints. Junctions are a *convoy pathfinding* feature and there is one convoy on one route. |
| **Road as a movement modifier** | **CUT FOREVER,** per the NO-SPEED RULE. Not deferred — forbidden. |
| **Jungle movement penalty** | A Pillar 1 decree wearing a roads costume. Separate wave, separate council. |
| **Mines / IEDs on roads** | The *most* thematically correct feature here and still a wave-2 cut. `scripts/combat/punji_trap.gd` and `claymore.gd` already exist as the substrate to reuse, so nothing is lost by waiting — and shipping mines in the same wave as roads means a playtest cannot tell us which one is wrong. |
| **Civilians / ox carts / refugees on roads** | Wave 2. **But bead it loudly** — this is the actual Rule #1 payload and it must not be forgotten because the road "shipped." |
| **Multiple road classes** | One class: dirt/laterite track. No highways, no paved, no trail-vs-road distinction. |
| **Road-following vegetation clearing beyond the minimum** | Suppress clutter/veg *on* the road surface and nothing more. No verge treatment, no sightline corridors. |
| **Authored road mesh geometry** | Shader mask + clutter suppression is enough for a PSX look, and the frame budget cannot be measured this session. |
| **Roads reaching every site** | 3–4 sites on the network is plenty. A village at the end of a footpath is *more* Vietnam than a village on a road. |

**One scope point that is a design requirement, not a technical one:** a **height-only road**
(briefing's Candidate C) is **design-dead.** A road the AI cannot perceive, the ambush planner
cannot score, the noise system cannot hear and the vegetation cannot avoid is a texture with a
groove in it. It would satisfy the letter of the wave and deliver none of it. Whatever storage Q1
chooses, the binding design requirement is: **"am I on / near a road?" must be answerable by the
player-noise path, the ambush planner, vegetation, and the convoy — through ONE authority.**

## Q4 · UNASKED BUT COUPLED — the map

Roads should be **printed on the topo sheet from mission start**, and this does **not** violate
ADR-022. The grease-pencil law splits *transient facts you witnessed* (OBSERVED, decays) from *your
beliefs* (ANNOTATED, never corrected). Permanent terrain is neither — it is **printed**, exactly as
the contour lines and rivers already are. A 1:50,000 sheet prints roads; a map that hid them would
be a fog-of-war overlay, which the ADR explicitly forbids.

The clean split, and it is the same split as Q3:
- **The road is PRINTED.** Permanent, from turn zero, free.
- **The traffic is OBSERVED.** You saw two trucks here Tuesday at dusk; it decays; it is the intel.
- **The ambush mark is ANNOTATED.** Grease pencil, may be wrong, never corrected.

Getting this wrong in the other direction — roads appearing on the sheet only once walked — is what
turns the map into a discovery checklist, which is closer to a quest log than printing them ever
was.

## TRADEOFFS NAMED (no free lunches)

- **The road will be underwhelming in wave 1.** With traffic cut, we ship a spine and a convoy. If
  the Summoner expects "the AO came alive," he will be disappointed. Say so before building.
- **The NO-SPEED RULE will feel wrong to players and to us.** Every instinct says a road should be
  faster. Holding the line means the road's benefit is *subtle* (orientation) while its cost is
  *loud* (exposure), and some players will simply never use it — which is, in fact, historically
  correct, and will still read to some as "why are there roads."
- **TRAFFIC_NEAR_M as a score, not a gate, means the truth-law complaint is only half-cured.** The
  constant becomes read, but the header's word "requirement" must be rewritten to "preference" in
  the same change or we have swapped one lie for another.
- **The Adjacency Rule constrains generation.** Roads cannot take the most dramatic line through
  terrain; anywhere the ground forces a single passage, the road must not go.
- **Cutting bridges means roads terminate at rivers,** which looks unfinished until fords are read
  as deliberate. Art and the topo sheet have to sell that.
- **Rule 5 has no probe.** "The road must remain the most dangerous ground" is playtest-only, and
  playtest gates are the slowest gates we have.

## MY VOTE

**BUILD IT — with the six rules written into the decree, TRAFFIC_NEAR_M as a score term rather than
a gate, and the wave-1 cut list above.**

The one thing I would push back on in the Summoner's framing: **"build the roads network" is not
the feature.** *"There is traffic in this AO"* is the feature; the road is its prerequisite. If the
wave ends when the polylines render, we will have spent the session on a texture and the convoy
will still have never moved. **The wave is not done until a truck drives down it.**
