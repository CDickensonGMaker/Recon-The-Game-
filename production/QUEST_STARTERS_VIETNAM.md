# Vietnam quests from Conquest of Worms — first pass

Source: `CONQUEST_OF_WORMS_AUTHOR_SYNOPSIS.md`, read 2026-09-09. Every quest below cites the line
it came from. Where I invented something it is labelled INVENTED. Nothing here is canon until Caleb
rules on it.

**Organising idea:** the chain is *mercy in one war becomes the thing that hunts the next*. So each
Vietnam quest has a WW1 twin the player eventually plays, and the twin should REFRAME the first
rather than repeat it.

**Scope:** post-demo-launch, per his 2026-09-09 ruling. Design now, build nothing yet.

---

## 1. SOUVENIRS
> "cutting ears off and wearing their ears around camp"

Not a cutscene about Eugene. The player patrols and the enemy dead start being wrong. The game
already has **bodies-give-intel-only** — so the player reads corpses, and the intel accruing is
about his OWN side. It accumulates over real patrols, in whatever order he walks them.

Then: report him, cover for him, or say nothing and keep finding them.

**WW1 twin:** the bully. The same cruelty, tolerated the same way — and it survives the war to
become the Legionnaire who creates the other enemy.

**Why it fits the game:** no rails, no trigger volume, works for a solo player, uses a shipped system.

---

## 2. THE SHELL HOLE
> "after hours of tlakign realizes that the dudes dead"

Pinned overnight with a wounded man. The conversation system runs. He answers. At some point his
answers stopped being real — and the game must let the player NOT NOTICE, because that is the horror.

This is Louie's beat transposed. When the player later plays Louie and it happens again in another
war, it lands as inheritance rather than repetition.

**RECOMMENDED FIRST BUILD.** Most drawable, most nearly buildable — the conversation system exists,
and the horror is entirely in what the player fails to notice.

**Open:** the author is unsure who is in the shell hole ("his commander or someone i cant remember").
The pages decide, not his summary.

---

## 3. HIS GROUND
> "starts to torture on the squad"

The skull-faced sniper is a persistent hunter, not a boss — which is what Pillar 3 is built for.
He has a signature: a shot placement, a body left in a posture. The player learns to read that a
place is his. He escalates across the campaign, is never on a schedule, and does not die when a
quest ends.

**WW1/WW2 twin:** post-demo, the player IS him being made. Every later encounter is with someone
he has played.

---

## 4. THE CARD
> "when a man dies you are left holding his card"
> (Michael Lee Crawford, b. 14 Aug 1950, Minden, Louisiana, registered 17 Aug 1967)

A man dies near the player and he ends up with the effects. The quest is what to do with them —
carry them, hand them to a clerk who will lose them, walk them to someone who knew him. Small,
solo, no combat required.

**WW1 twin:** the journal itself. Louie's effects reached his grandson. That is the only reason
any of this story exists.

**Note:** Pillar 4 asks for "minimal stats, maximal attachment". The draft card already IS that,
in his own art.

---

## 5. THE MERCY
> "stumbles across a young german soldier, but chooses to not shoot him"

The Vietnam-side echo. Someone in the player's sights who does not have to die. **It must be
genuinely possible to shoot, or the mercy is scenery.**

### RULED BY CALEB, 2026-09-09: **"even if they shoot the german, he gets away"**

The chain is unbreakable. Shoot or spare, the young German walks away, becomes the SS officer,
burns the Russian, and the skull-faced sniper hunts Michael either way.

**This is the strongest possible answer and it is thematically exact.** The war does not care what
the player chose. Mercy and murder produce the same man walking off through the smoke - which is
"Conquest of Worms" stated as a mechanic: you do not control the chain, you only inherit it.

**Where the consequence actually lives: THE JOURNAL.** The world outcome is fixed; the RECORD is
not. Michael inherits either a grandfather who spared a boy, or a grandfather who tried to kill him
and missed. Same sniper, different man reading about it in Vietnam. That is the payload, and it
costs no branching world state - only different journal text and a different Michael.

**Design consequences:**
- The shot must be able to MISS convincingly. A scripted miss the player can feel cheated by is
  worse than no choice; a man who is hit, staggers and still gets away is honest to war.
- Louie's journal entry is written by what the player did. That entry is what Michael reads.
- Nothing else in the world branches. One authored moment, two records, one chain.

---

## Two constraints that govern all of the above

**1. The horror rule is the hardest one.**
> "any horror gore scene in the comic is supposed to be representative of the psychological state
> of the person expericing the vision"

In-game horror therefore CANNOT be world state. Same corpse, different men, different image. That
is the nerve/suppression system, not a decal — and it is post-demo. So these quests must be built
so a horror layer DROPS INTO them later rather than requiring it now.

**2. Eugene must stay a person.**
He is crying, not melting — his own correction. The quests should keep him a man doing monstrous
things right to the end. Flatten him into a creature and the book's best idea is gone.
